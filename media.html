The Voiceless

Flagship Podcast

Long-form investigative journalism for your ears. Each season, The Voiceless goes deep on a single story that mainstream media won't touch — following the evidence, centering the people affected, and holding power to account. Hosted by Amara Okafor.

EP 24

The Landlord Algorithm: How AI Is Deciding Who Gets to Live Where

March 24, 202648 min

Major corporate landlords are using AI tenant screening tools that systematically reject applicants based on postal code, social media activity, and credit patterns that correlate with race and income. We obtained the algorithms. The results are damning.

EP 23

The Mercury Legacy: A Northern Community's 60-Year Fight for Justice

March 10, 202655 min

Grassy Narrows First Nation has been fighting mercury contamination since the 1960s. The government knew. The company knew. Nobody acted. Now a new generation is leading the fight — and they're winning.

EP 22

Ghost Kitchens: The Workers Behind Canada's Food Delivery Boom

February 24, 202642 min

Inside the windowless kitchens cooking your delivery orders. We follow workers through 14-hour shifts in facilities with no ventilation, no breaks, and no employment protections. The companies say they're "partners." The workers say they're trapped.

EP 21

The Vanishing Schools: How Consolidation Is Killing Rural Canada

February 10, 202651 min

When a town loses its school, it loses its future. We visit five communities across three provinces where school closures have triggered population collapse, economic decline, and a grief no one talks about.

EP 20

Detained Without Charge: Inside Canada's Immigration Holding Centres

January 27, 202659 min

Canada detains asylum seekers in provincial jails alongside criminal populations. Some have been held for years with no charge and no trial date. We speak to five people who lived it — and the lawyers fighting to end the practice.

EP 19

The Pill Mill Papers: How a Small-Town Pharmacy Fuelled an Opioid Crisis

January 13, 202646 min

Leaked dispensing records reveal a single pharmacy in Thunder Bay filled over 80,000 opioid prescriptions in one year. The pharmacist drove a Porsche. The neighbourhood buried its children. A story of greed, negligence, and a regulatory system that looked away.
