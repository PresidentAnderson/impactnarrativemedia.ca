Elder Margaret "Maggie" Beardy

1938 – 2026 · Muskrat Dam First Nation, ON

Elder Margaret Beardy walked on peacefully on March 18, surrounded by four generations of her family. A keeper of the Oji-Cree language and traditional medicine, Maggie spent her life ensuring that the old ways would never be lost. She raised 8 children, fostered 12 more, and was grandmother to a community. She taught language classes until she was 85, insisting every child should know the words their ancestors spoke.

"She could tell you the name of every plant in the bush, what it healed, and the story behind it," said her grandson David Beardy. "When she left, a library burned."

Lives Remembered

Trần Văn Minh (known as "Uncle Minh")

1945 – 2026 · Calgary, AB

Boat person, community builder, beloved restaurateur. Uncle Minh arrived in Canada in 1979 with nothing and built Saigon Star, the restaurant that became the heart of Calgary's Vietnamese community for 40 years. He sponsored 23 families to come to Canada. His phở recipe died with him, but his legacy lives in every family he helped build a new life.

Riley Chen-Nakamura

1998 – 2026 · Vancouver, BC

Queer youth advocate, community organizer, and relentless force for change. Riley founded the Rainbow Youth Alliance at UBC and led the campaign for gender-neutral washrooms across Vancouver schools. They volunteered at Qmunity and organized mutual aid during COVID. Riley is mourned by their parents, their partner Alex, and the hundreds of young people whose lives they made brighter.

Helen "Miss Helen" Chicken

1940 – 2026 · Fredericton, NB

Forty-two years in the classroom. Every child who passed through Room 204 at George Street Middle School knew Miss Helen's rules: "Be kind, be curious, be brave." She kept in touch with hundreds of former students and attended their weddings, graduations, and first days of teaching. She is survived by her sister Dorothy, three cats, and an entire city of people who learned to read because of her.

Angus "Red" MacNeil

1951 – 2026 · Chéticamp, NS

The sea took Red's father and two brothers, but it never took his love for the water. A lobster fisherman for 48 seasons, Red knew every rock and current along the Cape Breton coast. He served as volunteer fire chief for 20 years, played fiddle at every ceilidh, and never met a stranger. His boat, the Mary Grace, will be retired in his honour.

Rose Couture-Flett

1933 – 2026 · St. Laurent, MB

Métis matriarch, bead worker, keeper of stories. Grandma Rose raised 6 children on the shores of Lake Manitoba and taught every one of them to jig, make bannock, and never forget where they came from. Her beadwork hangs in the Manitoba Museum. She spoke Michif until her last day, telling her great-grandchildren, "This language is your inheritance. Don't let anyone take it."
