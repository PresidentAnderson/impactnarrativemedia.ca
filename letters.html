Letters to the Editor

A newspaper belongs to its readers. These pages are yours.

The letters page has always been the soul of a newspaper. It's where readers talk back, push back, add context, share what we missed, and tell us what matters. We believe journalism is a conversation, not a lecture.

Our commitment: We publish letters from all perspectives, with priority given to voices typically excluded from mainstream discourse.

Letter of the Week

Housing Crisis

Re: "The Invisible Eviction" — I am one of the 40,000

I read your investigation with my hands shaking. My family was one of the families in your data. In 2024, our landlord — a numbered company I later discovered was owned by a pension fund — served us a renoviction notice on our apartment in Parkdale. We had lived there for 14 years. My daughter took her first steps in that hallway. We fought it at the Landlord and Tenant Board, but the system is designed to exhaust you. We lost.

Today we pay $800 more per month for a smaller apartment 45 minutes from my daughter's school. The "renovations" to our old unit? A coat of paint and new cabinet handles. It's now listed at $2,400. This isn't a housing market. It's a wealth extraction machine, and regular Canadians are the resource being mined.

Thank you for counting us. For the first time, I felt like someone saw what happened.

Sandra Mikhailova, Toronto, ONMarch 25, 2026

Healthcare Access March 24, 2026

Rural healthcare isn't a provincial problem — it's a national emergency

I'm a nurse practitioner in northern New Brunswick and the situation here mirrors everything in your Saskatchewan investigation. We have one doctor for 4,000 people. Last month a man died of a heart attack because the nearest ER was 90 minutes away. The ambulance took 40 minutes. This is a national crisis being treated as a provincial inconvenience. When will Ottawa stop passing the buck?

Michel Thibodeau, RN(NP), Campbellton, NB
Indigenous Rights March 23, 2026

Clean water is not a luxury — it is a treaty right

Your investigation into drinking water advisories hit close to home. I am from Neskantaga First Nation, where we have been under a boil-water advisory since 1995. That is 31 years. I was born under this advisory. My children were born under it. We are not waiting for clean water — we are being denied it. Every broken promise is a violation of our treaty rights. We ask Canadians: would you accept this for your family? For 31 years?

Crystal Moonias, Neskantaga First Nation, ON
Climate Action March 22, 2026

We rebuilt after the flood. Then it happened again. And again.

Three floods in five years. Our home in the Sumas Prairie has been destroyed twice and damaged once. Insurance won't cover us anymore. The government offers disaster relief that takes years to arrive. Meanwhile, the companies responsible for the emissions driving these floods post record profits. When do we stop calling these "natural" disasters? There is nothing natural about this.

Wei and Jennifer Lin, Abbotsford, BC
Education Funding March 21, 2026

Our children deserve better than crumbling schools and burned-out teachers

I am a parent and a school council member in Scarborough. Our school has mould in the walls, no air conditioning, and class sizes of 32. Our teachers are exhausted and leaving the profession in record numbers. The province announces education "investments" that don't even cover inflation. Meanwhile, my children's school can't afford to replace broken windows. We are failing an entire generation.

Priya Rajapaksa, Scarborough, ON
Rural Broadband March 20, 2026

In 2026, my kids still can't do homework because we have no internet

We live 40 minutes from Sudbury and our internet maxes out at 5 Mbps on a good day. My children drive to the Tim Hortons parking lot to submit their school assignments. The federal government has spent billions on rural broadband. Where did the money go? Certainly not here. We are being left behind, and the digital divide is becoming a canyon.

Marc-André Dupuis, Noëlville, ON
Immigration Policy March 19, 2026

I came here to build a life. The system is designed to keep me temporary.

I arrived in Canada on a work permit five years ago. I pay taxes, I volunteer, my children go to school here, and I contribute to my community. But the path to permanent residency has become a maze designed to keep people like me in limbo — working, paying, contributing, but never belonging. Temporary foreign workers built this country's economy through the pandemic. When will Canada see us as people, not labour units?

Name withheld by request, Kelowna, BC
Disability Rights March 18, 2026

The Canada Disability Benefit is not enough to survive on. That is the point.

When the Canada Disability Benefit was announced, many of us cried with relief. Then we saw the amount: $200 per month. In a country where rent alone exceeds $1,500 in most cities, this is not a benefit — it is a message. The message is that disabled lives are worth less. I live on $1,200 a month total. After rent, I have $47 for food, medicine, and everything else. I am not living. I am rationing my existence. Is this the Canada we want?

Jordan Kirkpatrick, Vancouver, BC
Housing Crisis March 17, 2026

I'm a doctor. I can't afford to live in the city where I treat patients.

I am a family physician in Vancouver. I make a good salary by any reasonable standard. And I cannot afford to buy a home in the city where I work. I commute 90 minutes each way. If a doctor can't afford to live here, what hope does anyone else have? The housing crisis isn't just about affordability — it's about what kind of cities we're building. Cities where only the wealthy can stay are not communities. They are gated compounds.

Dr. Amira Hassan, Vancouver, BC
